Is Your Home Ready for Christmas? Essential Appliance Tips

Christmas asks a lot of your appliances all at once — cooking, extra dishes, guest laundry, all in the span of a few days. A little prep beforehand goes a long way toward avoiding a breakdown at the worst possible moment.

Quick Answer

Test your oven's temperature accuracy a week ahead, vacuum refrigerator coils, clean the dishwasher filter, check washer hoses, and clear the dryer vent — all before guests arrive. This single prep pass prevents most Christmas-week breakdown calls.

Your Oven Needs a Dress Rehearsal

Before the big meal, test your oven's actual temperature with a simple thermometer — many run 25–50 degrees off from the dial without anyone noticing. Skip self-cleaning mode right before a big cooking day; the extreme heat it generates can occasionally cause sensors or thermal fuses to fail. Check the door seal too, since a loose gasket lets heat escape and causes uneven cooking.

3×
Home cooking fires are roughly three times more likely around major holiday cooking days, according to the National Fire Protection Association. A pre-holiday oven and stove check is one of the simplest ways to reduce that risk.

Your Fridge Can't Handle Being Overstuffed

Blocked air vents force a fridge to work much harder. Leave room for air to circulate, keep the fridge between 37–40°F and the freezer at 0°F, and take five minutes to vacuum the condenser coils — a small task that meaningfully reduces strain on the compressor. Our refrigerator maintenance guide covers the full routine.

Dishwashers Need Help Keeping Up

Clean the filter before the holiday dish marathon begins, run hot water at the sink before starting a cycle, and avoid overloading racks in a way that blocks the spray arms.

Pro Tip

Run your dishwasher's cleaning cycle a few days before guests arrive, not the morning of. It needs to run empty, which is much easier to schedule ahead of the holiday dish rush.

Washing Machines Need Lighter Loads With Guests Around

More people means more laundry, but overloaded washers are a common source of holiday breakdowns. Check hoses for wear, and keep loads balanced to avoid strain on the motor and suspension.

Dryers Are a Fire Hazard When Lint Builds Up

Clean the lint trap after every load, and confirm the outside vent flap opens properly when the dryer runs. If drying times suddenly increase, that's usually a sign of an airflow problem worth addressing before it becomes a bigger issue.

Garbage Disposals Need Respect During Meal Prep

Avoid putting potato peels, fibrous vegetables, pasta, rice, eggshells, bones, or grease down the disposal — these are the most common causes of holiday clogs. Running cold water for 15 seconds before and after use helps move debris through safely.

Pro Tip

Keep a strainer over the disposal drain during heavy prep days. It's a simple habit that catches most of the debris that would otherwise cause a holiday-week clog.

Having a Backup Plan

Even with good prep, appliances can occasionally fail at inconvenient times. Having a local repair company's number saved ahead of the season, and a general sense of contingencies (a neighbor's oven, a nearby laundromat) gives real peace of mind if something does go sideways on the day itself.

When to Call a Professional

Strange noises, burning smells, a fridge that isn't holding temperature, or a washer shaking violently are all signs worth addressing before the holiday rush, not during it. A little attention now means one less thing to worry about when the guests arrive.

Older Ovens Face More Risk Under Holiday Loads

An oven's heating element and thermostat degrade gradually over years of use, and the sustained high-heat demands of holiday cooking — hours-long roasts, back-to-back baking — put more stress on an aging unit than typical everyday cooking does. If your oven is already older, the pre-holiday temperature check described above is worth taking especially seriously, since it's the appliance most likely to reveal age-related wear under this kind of extended use.

Mind Your Kitchen's Electrical Circuit Load

Running an oven alongside multiple countertop appliances — a slow cooker, an electric griddle, a coffee maker — simultaneously can strain a kitchen circuit, particularly in older homes with more limited electrical capacity. If you notice a breaker tripping during heavy holiday cooking, that's worth addressing before the next big cooking day, either by redistributing appliances across circuits or having an electrician evaluate whether the kitchen's wiring needs an upgrade.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I test my oven before Christmas dinner?

At least a week ahead, using an oven thermometer to confirm accuracy. This gives enough time to schedule a repair if something's off, rather than discovering it the morning of.

Is it safe to run my oven's self-clean cycle right before Christmas?

It's best avoided in the days immediately before a big cooking day. The extreme heat occasionally causes a sensor or thermal fuse to fail — run it a few weeks ahead instead if needed.

What items should never go down the garbage disposal during holiday cooking?

Potato peels, fibrous vegetables like celery and onion skins, pasta, rice, eggshells, bones, and grease or cooking oil. These are the most common causes of holiday clogs and breakdowns.

How can I prevent my fridge from struggling with holiday food overload?

Avoid blocking air vents when packing it full, leave some room between items for airflow, and keep the temperature at 37–40°F. Vacuum the coils beforehand so the compressor isn't already working harder than it needs to.

What's the risk of an overloaded washer during guest season?

Overloaded or unbalanced loads strain the motor and suspension, and are one of the most common causes of holiday washer breakdowns from the surge in guest laundry.

How do I keep my dishwasher running smoothly through multiple holiday meals in one day?

Scrape rather than fully pre-rinse dishes to avoid overworking the filter with excess debris, avoid overloading racks so spray arms can move freely, and if running back-to-back loads, let the unit complete its full cycle rather than interrupting it.

Should I have a backup plan if an appliance fails on Christmas Day itself?

Knowing a local repair company's emergency contact information ahead of time, and having a general sense of which neighbors or family members might lend a working appliance temporarily, are both reasonable low-effort contingencies worth having in mind.

Do older ovens face more risk during heavy holiday use than newer ones?

Generally yes — older heating elements and thermostats have had more time to degrade, making an older oven somewhat more likely to show issues under the sustained high-heat use that holiday cooking demands.

How much extra load can a typical kitchen electrical circuit handle during holiday cooking?

This varies significantly by home and circuit age, which is exactly why running multiple high-draw kitchen appliances simultaneously (oven, multiple countertop appliances) is worth being mindful of, particularly in older homes with more limited circuit capacity.
