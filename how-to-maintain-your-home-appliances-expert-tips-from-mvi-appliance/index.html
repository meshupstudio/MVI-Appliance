How to Maintain Your Home Appliances: Expert Tips From MVI Appliance

Well-maintained appliances are the backbone of a smoothly running household. None of the habits below take much time, but together they add up to meaningfully fewer repair calls — especially in a climate as demanding on appliances as Fresno's.

Quick Answer

Clean refrigerator coils twice a year, dishwasher filters monthly, dryer lint traps every load, and washer gaskets weekly. These four habits alone prevent the majority of appliance service calls we see in Fresno and Clovis.

Keep Your Refrigerator Running Efficiently

Clean the condenser coils every six months — dust buildup forces the compressor to work harder than it should. Check the door seals periodically, since a loose seal lets cool air escape and wastes energy. Keep the fridge at or below 40°F and the freezer at 0°F. Our full refrigerator maintenance guide covers this in more depth.

Ensure Your Dishwasher Cleans Effectively

Remove and rinse the filter regularly to prevent clogs, and run the kitchen faucet until the water's hot before starting a cycle, so the dishwasher begins with hot water rather than heating it from cold. Use a detergent suited to your water hardness to avoid mineral buildup — Fresno's hard water makes this more important than in many other regions.

Pro Tip

Run a monthly cleaning cycle with a dishwasher-safe descaler. Fresno's hard water leaves mineral film on interior components faster than in areas with softer water, and this one habit noticeably extends performance.

Maintain Your Washing Machine

Avoid overloading, which strains the motor and reduces cleaning performance. Use high-efficiency detergent if you have an HE machine. Wipe down the drum and rubber gasket regularly to prevent mold and mildew.

Keep Your Dryer Safe and Efficient

Clean the lint filter after every single load — this one habit does more for fire safety than almost anything else. Periodically inspect the venting system for obstructions, and pay attention to unusual noises, which often signal worn parts before they fail completely.

Refrigerator water filters should be replaced roughly every 6 months in Fresno, compared to the sometimes longer intervals suggested in areas with softer water. Hard water shortens filter effectiveness meaningfully.

A Realistic Monthly Time Commitment

Homeowners often assume a full maintenance routine takes hours. In reality, spread across a month, it's closer to 30–45 minutes total — a few minutes wiping a gasket here, ten minutes cleaning coils twice a year, seconds clearing a lint trap after each load. It adds up to meaningfully less time than dealing with a single unexpected breakdown.

Adjusting for Household Size

A larger household running appliances more frequently should lean toward the more frequent end of any range mentioned in this guide. More laundry cycles mean faster lint buildup; more dishwasher loads mean faster filter clogging; more refrigerator door-opening means the coils and seals work harder. Scale your routine to your actual usage rather than a one-size-fits-all schedule.

Schedule Regular Maintenance

Routine professional maintenance extends the lifespan of your appliances and helps catch small issues before they become expensive repairs. It's easy to put off until something breaks — but it's meaningfully cheaper to stay ahead of it.

Pro Tip

Bundle your appliance maintenance into a single seasonal visit rather than scattering it throughout the year. It's easier to remember, and a technician doing a full-home visit often catches cross-appliance issues a single-appliance check would miss.

The Habits That Shorten Appliance Life the Most

Across the households we service, three habits consistently show up as the biggest contributors to premature appliance failure: chronically overloading washers and dishwashers beyond their rated capacity, letting small noises or minor leaks go unaddressed for weeks or months, and simply skipping filter and coil cleaning altogether rather than falling behind schedule. None of these require expensive tools or specialized knowledge to avoid — they're purely a matter of consistent attention.

Making Maintenance a Habit That Actually Sticks

The maintenance tasks most likely to get skipped are the ones without a natural trigger reminding you to do them. Anchoring tasks to existing routines helps: clean the lint trap every time you start a load, since that's already part of your laundry routine. For less frequent tasks like coil cleaning, tying them to an obvious seasonal marker — the start of summer, the New Year — makes them far easier to remember than relying on a vague sense of "it's been a while."

A few consistent habits, spread across all your major appliances, go a long way toward keeping your home running the way it should.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the easiest appliance maintenance task to start with?

Cleaning your dryer's lint trap after every load. It takes seconds, prevents fire risk, and is the single easiest habit to build since it's already part of most laundry routines.

How much time does a full appliance maintenance routine take per month?

Realistically, 30–45 minutes spread across the month covers coil cleaning, filter checks, and gasket wiping for most households — a small time investment relative to the repair costs it prevents.

Does MVI Appliance offer a maintenance program?

Yes, MVI Appliance offers scheduled maintenance visits covering refrigerators, dishwashers, washers, and dryers throughout Fresno, Clovis, and Madera.

What's the biggest maintenance mistake homeowners make?

Waiting for a problem to appear before doing any maintenance at all. Most major appliance failures are preventable with basic, regular attention rather than reactive fixes after something's already gone wrong.

Can good maintenance actually lower my utility bills?

Yes. Appliances working against dust, worn seals, or clogged filters use meaningfully more energy and water than well-maintained ones, so consistent upkeep shows up directly on monthly utility costs.

Should maintenance frequency change based on household size?

Yes — larger households running appliances more frequently should lean toward the more frequent end of any maintenance range mentioned here, since more use naturally means faster buildup of dust, lint, and residue.

Is it worth hiring a professional for tasks I could do myself?

For simple tasks, DIY is perfectly reasonable. The value in a professional maintenance visit is the trained eye catching early-stage issues — a slightly worn belt, a fan that's starting to struggle — that aren't obvious during routine cleaning.

What household habits accidentally shorten appliance life the most?

Overloading washers and dishwashers, ignoring small noises or leaks, and skipping filter and coil cleaning entirely are the three habits that most consistently shorten appliance lifespan across the households we service.

How do I build these habits into a routine I'll actually keep up with?

Anchoring tasks to existing habits works well — clean the lint trap every time you start a load (already part of the routine), and pair less frequent tasks like coil cleaning with a seasonal marker like the start of summer or the New Year.
